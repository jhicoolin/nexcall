NEXCALL - UX RESEARCH NOTES
Last updated: May 13, 2026

Goal:
Make the page easier to understand, easier to trust, and easier to act on without using manipulative dark patterns.

Design differentiation note:
The current homepage intentionally avoids the default AI SaaS pattern of "centered hero, logo strip, feature grid, pricing." The first visual now behaves like a front-office command room: live calls, diverse operators/customers, call path, and operational handoff. The next section continues the same idea as a signal map instead of another generic feature block.

============================================================
1. FINAL SECTION ORDER
============================================================

1. Hero
   Purpose: immediate value proposition, primary phone CTA, and demo CTA.
   UX reason: users need to know what the product is, who it is for, and what action to take within seconds.

2. Trust signal bar
   Purpose: answer the first credibility questions quickly: calendar, fallback, phone routing, checkout, current review.
   UX reason: Stanford Web Credibility research says credibility improves when information is verifiable, contact/action paths are clear, and the site feels useful and professional.

3. Use-case strip
   Purpose: help different buyer types recognize themselves.
   UX reason: information scent improves when navigation labels match the visitor's task vocabulary.

4. Problem snapshot
   Purpose: name the buyer's pain before explaining features.
   UX reason: problem recognition raises motivation, but the copy stays specific and honest.

5. Front-office signal map
   Purpose: show the workflow as a live operational handoff.
   UX reason: concrete process visuals reduce ambiguity and help buyers picture the product in their own business.

6. Voice demos
   Purpose: prove the product concept early with realistic scenarios.
   UX reason: demos reduce uncertainty and mental effort. Users do not need to imagine what "AI receptionist" means.

7. Jobs done
   Purpose: explain the product in plain outcomes: answer, book, follow up.
   UX reason: lower cognitive load by grouping features into three memorable jobs.

8. How it works
   Purpose: show a simple implementation path.
   UX reason: the Fogg Behavior Model says action requires motivation, ability, and prompt. This section increases ability by making setup feel manageable.

9. Human proof
   Purpose: add warmth and social proof after the user understands the offer.
   UX reason: trust and belonging matter, but proof works better after the visitor understands the product.

10. Industry playbooks
   Purpose: show the system adapts beyond one niche.
   UX reason: visitors look for category fit before buying.

11. Integrations
    Purpose: answer "will this work with my tools?"
    UX reason: strong information scent and concrete system names reduce uncertainty.

12. What actually works
    Purpose: handle major buyer objections: calendar logic, human fallback, accents, predictable pricing.
    UX reason: objection handling before pricing reduces anxiety.

13. Pricing
    Purpose: show three options with one recommended middle plan.
    UX reason: choice overload research supports limited choices. Three tiers are enough to compare without overwhelming.

14. FAQ
    Purpose: answer final objections before the form.
    UX reason: keeps checkout/demo intent from leaking into uncertainty.

15. Lead capture
    Purpose: collect only the minimum needed to send a useful demo.
    UX reason: shorter forms and visible progress reduce friction.

2. RESEARCH USED
============================================================

Stanford Web Credibility Guidelines
https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/index.html

Key application:
- Make contact and next steps easy.
- Show real organization signals.
- Keep copy clear and sincere.
- Avoid unsupported claims.
- Avoid small errors and broken links.

Fogg Behavior Model - Stanford Behavior Design Lab
https://behaviordesign.stanford.edu/resources/fogg-behavior-model

Key application:
- Increase motivation with concrete pain and outcome.
- Increase ability with a simple four-step rollout.
- Add prompts after high-confidence sections: hero, demo, how-it-works, pricing, final form.

Choice Overload - Iyengar and Lepper
https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/when-choice-demotivating-can-one-desire-too-much-good-thing

Key application:
- Keep pricing to three plans.
- Make the middle plan the recommended starting point.
- Avoid overwhelming the visitor with every possible feature before the main decision.

Primacy and Recency Effects on Clicking Behavior
https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/11/2/522/4617731

Key application:
- Put high-intent navigation items early and late.
- Header now prioritizes Demos first and keeps the direct CTA visually separate.

Information Scent / Information Foraging
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221517585_Using_Information_Scent_to_Model_User_Information_Needs_and_Actions_on_the_Web

Key application:
- Use specific labels such as Demos, How It Works, Stories, Pricing, FAQ.
- Use buyer vocabulary: booking, SMS, calendar, CRM, human fallback, call summary.

MIT Sloan trust in e-business study
https://ideas.repec.org/p/mit/sloanp/1826.html

Key application:
- Trust cues need to appear before payment or contact forms.
- Avoid exaggerated security/compliance claims unless the business has them.

Cognitive load and human-centered design
https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/human-centered-design-meets-cognitive-load-theory-designing-inter

Key application:
- Break complex setup into small sections.
- Use repeated card patterns and short labels.
- Keep paragraphs scannable and avoid feature dumps.

Baymard checkout usability research
https://wislibrary.org/library/baymard-institute-e-commerce-checkout-usability/

Key application:
- Keep checkout hosted by Stripe.
- Make checkout button labels explicit.
- Do not force account creation before payment.
- Make errors visible when checkout is not configured.

============================================================
3. ETHICAL PERSUASION RULES
============================================================

Use:
- Clarity
- Social proof
- Specific outcomes
- Fewer choices
- Honest risk reduction
- Clear next steps

Avoid:
- Fake urgency
- Fake scarcity
- Fake testimonials
- Unsupported compliance claims
- "No hallucinations" guarantees
- Sponsored logos without permission
