5. TWILIO SETUP
============================================================

Twilio is used for the public phone number.

What to buy:

1. Buy and manage AI receptionist numbers in your agency Twilio account.
2. Put the public demo number in:

NEXT_PUBLIC_DEMO_PHONE_NUMBER="+1YOURTWILIONUMBER"

3. In Twilio, configure incoming calls:

A call comes in:
Webhook
POST
https://your-domain.com/api/twilio/voice

4. In this app, /api/twilio/voice checks the Postgres tenant database and routes calls according to that tenant's voice provider.

If no matching client is found, it forwards calls to:

VOICE_AGENT_WEBHOOK_URL

That URL comes from your voice agent platform.

For client launches, assign the client an AI receptionist number from your Twilio pool. If they already have a business number, they can forward missed, overflow, after-hours, or all calls into the assigned AI number. They only need their own Twilio account if they want to own the carrier account directly.

If you use the native live-call engine instead of an external voice-agent provider:

1. Deploy scripts/twilio-media-server.mjs to a WebSocket-capable host.
2. Set TWILIO_MEDIA_STREAM_URL to its secure WSS URL.
3. Set AI_TURN_ENDPOINT to your Vercel /api/ai/respond route.
4. Set matching AI_TURN_API_TOKEN values in Vercel and the media server host.
5. Add each client's systemPrompt, voice provider, phone routing, and calendar rules in the admin dashboard.

5. Configure incoming SMS:

A message comes in:
Webhook
POST
Your Zapier/Make/Twilio Studio SMS workflow URL

Recommended SMS workflows:

Customer confirmation:
"You're booked for {time}. Reply here if anything changes."

Team alert:
"New call: {name}, {phone}, {need}, {appointment_time}, {summary}."

Important:
Use HTTPS in production.
Twilio recommends validating webhook signatures before trusting production traffic.

Good for no-code/enterprise-style workflows and integrations.

Good for pathway-style call flows and bundled voice infrastructure.

Website demo voice setup:

The site now supports three voice layers:

1. Uploaded audio clips
   Best for public launch. Generate or record one polished MP3 per scenario, put files in public/audio, and set the NEXT_PUBLIC_DEMO_AUDIO_* values.

2. Optional ElevenLabs TTS generation
   Route: app/api/tts/elevenlabs/route.ts
   Add ELEVENLABS_API_KEY, ELEVENLABS_DEMO_VOICE_ID, and set NEXT_PUBLIC_ENABLE_ELEVENLABS_TTS_DEMOS="true" to let the demo create scenario audio server-side.
   The route accepts scenario IDs only and uses approved server-side scenario scripts. It rejects custom text so humanized voice generation is not used for the live chat or arbitrary visitor prompts.
   Keep this disabled for paid traffic until latency, reliability, and phone-speaker quality are tested.

3. Browser fallback voices
   The demo automatically picks the most natural installed browser voice it can find for each scenario. It uses different male/female voice profiles depending on the call type.

Recommended public-site voice profiles:

- Appointment Scheduling: warm female voice, calm healthcare tone.
- Lead Qualification: confident male voice, consultative but not pushy.
- Restaurant Concierge: bright female voice, polished hospitality tone.
- Customer Support: calm male voice, patient support tone.
- AI IVR / Financial Routing: steady male voice, precise and trustworthy.
- Legal Intake: empathetic female voice, slower and reassuring.

Recommended ElevenLabs launch setup:

1. Create one primary NexCall demo voice for booking calls.
2. Optionally create separate voice IDs for lead qualification and support.
3. Store voice IDs in Vercel as ELEVENLABS_DEMO_VOICE_ID, ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID_LEAD, and ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID_SUPPORT.
4. Keep ELEVENLABS_API_KEY server-side only.

Read VOICE_LAB_RESEARCH.txt only as legacy research notes.

Recommended voice quality path:

1. Test your ElevenLabs and voice-agent provider voices by phone, not only laptop speakers.
2. Pick one male and one female voice style that sound calm on a phone speaker.

Patterns from successful AI receptionist companies:

1. Do not sell "AI" alone.
   Sell the outcome: answered calls, booked appointments, routed inquiries, follow-up, summaries.

2. Demos matter.
   Successful sites show scenario-based demos by industry: restaurant, healthcare, legal, support, real estate, appointment booking.

3. Flat pricing feels safer.
   Small businesses dislike unpredictable per-minute billing. The current site uses flat monthly plans and call limits.

4. Human fallback matters.
   The strongest offers include escalation for complex or sensitive situations.

5. Calendar integration is non-negotiable.
   Booking, rescheduling, cancellation, buffers, and appointment types must work before pitching the product as real.

6. Integrations sell confidence.
   Buyers want to see calendars, CRMs, phone systems, SMS, and workflow tools.

7. Business-type specificity matters.
   The site now includes industry cards and scenario demos instead of generic AI language.

8. Trust language must be careful.
   Do not claim SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR, uptime guarantees, or sponsor logos unless you actually have them.

9. Voice quality and accent handling are major concerns.
   Test with noisy calls, accents, interruptions, phone-quality audio, and caller corrections.

10. Hybrid is the safest launch path.
   Start with AI for repeatable calls and route complex calls to a person.
